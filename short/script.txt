The greatest thief in the city was finally caught. He was putting things back.

They say it happened in a crooked little town of chimneys and cobblestones.

Every night, something went missing. A clock. A kettle. A child's music box.

The mayor lost his favourite chair.

People bolted their doors. They nailed their windows shut. One man slept inside his own cupboard.

The guards chased shadows across the rooftops for a whole year.

But then people noticed something strange. Everything came back.

The clock came back ticking. The kettle came back whistling. The music box played a song it had forgotten years ago.

So one night, the captain of the guard hid in the mayor's house and waited.

At midnight, the window creaked open. A masked figure climbed in, carrying a chair.

The whole town came running with torches.

You see, the thief had never wanted their things. He only took what was broken.

He carried it home, mended it by candlelight, and brought it back before morning.

And nobody in that town ever opened the door to a stranger.

The town held a trial. The verdict took one minute.

They sentenced him to a workshop on the main street, with his name above the door.

And he never had to steal a broken thing again. People just left them on his step.
